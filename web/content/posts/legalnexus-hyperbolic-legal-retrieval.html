What this note covers

The paper’s full text is not public yet, so this note sticks to what its title, venue and status commit to, plus background on the ideas involved. Background and schematics are labelled as such; no result from the paper is quoted or implied. The preprint page could not be read from my environment, so for the abstract and results please follow the SSRN link.

Legal research is a retrieval problem with structure. A case is not just a document; it sits inside a web of citations, within a hierarchy of courts and statutes. A retriever that treats every case as an unrelated vector discards exactly the structure a lawyer relies on.

Two ideas in the title

Hierarchy-aware, hyperbolic. Embedding a hierarchy in ordinary Euclidean space distorts it as the tree grows; hyperbolic space has the right amount of room. Graph neural networks over such embeddings can keep high-level legal concepts and specific cases at suitable distances, so that “near” can mean “related, at the right level of authority”.

Multi-agent refinement. Retrieved cases can contradict each other or be only superficially similar. Letting several agents critique and repair the candidate set is the same instinct as in my EVIRAG work: when sources conflict, resolve the conflict explicitly instead of hiding it.

Link to the research direction

This paper sits on the “knowledge side” of the direction I am moving toward: structured, hierarchical knowledge (here a citation and court hierarchy) giving language models a more explainable basis for decisions.
